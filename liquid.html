Skoden

A look at how Skoden is put together, from the full assembly down to the avionics and engine. Each part below includes an exploded-view animation showing how the pieces fit together.

Airframe

The Skoden rocket’s airframe is engineered to fit within a strict 5-inch geometric envelope, designed to optimize aerodynamic performance while maintaining structural integrity for a target apogee of 10 km. The vehicle measures 2675 mm in length and features a 4-fin configuration, with fin root cords of 275 mm and a height of 125 mm. The total dry mass of the vehicle is 16.9 kg, increasing to a wet mass of 28.1 kg when fully loaded.

The primary structure utilizes 5-inch outer diameter 6061-T6 aluminum tubing, with a thickness of 0.125 inches. To address thermal loads between the engine casing and the composite PA12 GF fincan, the team implemented a 6061-T6 radially-propagating structure around the phenolic base. This design modification serves two purposes: it creates necessary thermal separation between layers and reduces the phenolic chamber mass by 54% to 1.32 kg. This structural change significantly improved the vehicle’s static stability margin to 2.85 cal.

The airframe is highly integrated, housing the concentric autogenously pressurized tanks and the propulsion manifold systems. During the Design for Manufacturing and Assembly (DFMA) assessment, the team optimized the internal layout, notably eliminating hose clamps between the injector manifold and the concentric tanks' bottom bulkhead.

To ensure the reliability of the pressurized systems, the team developed comprehensive Geometrical Dimensioning and Tolerancing (GD&T) specifications. These studies, informed by chemical compatibility data and Parker O-Ring guides, dictate surface finish requirements for all fluidic manifolds to prevent boiloff and leakage. The structure has been validated through high-fidelity structural and thermal simulations, ensuring the airframe withstands aerodynamic, thermal, and dynamic loads across the entire flight domain. The result is a robust, lightweight structure capable of supporting the high thrust-to-weight ratio required for the Skoden's mission objectives.

avionics

The Skoden rocket’s avionics and communication architecture is designed to ensure robust telemetry, command, and control throughout the mission, leveraging a combination of long-range wireless protocols and onboard automated controllers. The telemetry and transmission system relies on a LoRaWAN communication architecture operating on 2.4 GHz RF channels, facilitating a reliable link between the onboard systems and Ground Support Equipment (GSE) receiver PCs. This setup is critical for monitoring the vehicle's state in real-time, including pressure, temperature, and battery telemetry during both pre-flight fill procedures and flight operations.

Control of the rocket's propulsion and fluidic systems is managed by an on-board Propulsion Thermalfluidic Systems Controller. This unit is integrated into the vehicle’s flight systems to manage critical operational states. The architecture employs a robust sensor suite that feeds data into this controller, allowing for dynamic monitoring of nitrous oxide mass, ethanol levels, and system pressures. Safety is further bolstered by the integration of line cutters for CO2 actuation, ensuring the vehicle can be safely managed or dumped in the event of an anomaly.

The recovery system architecture is intrinsically linked to the avionics suite, which is tasked with initiating the dual-break recovery sequence at the appropriate apogee. To ensure precise post-flight recovery and data analysis, the system includes official altitude logging and continuous rocket tracking transmissions. The integration of high-fidelity sensors and stable RF links allows the team to perform 3-sigma Monte Carlo simulations of the landing zone, mapping flight trajectories and ensuring that the recovery system can reliably pinpoint landing locations. By using LoraWAN and HAM radio channels, the avionics suite provides redundancy in communication, ensuring that critical telemetry reaches the ground team throughout the flight and descent phases, ultimately validating the rocket's performance and supporting post-flight data analysis.

engine

Skoden’s liquid engine uses a fuel centred coaxial shear injector with 44 elements. Stainless steel hypodermic needles are bonded to the injector with DP 420 epoxy, with the ethanol line running above, and a radial distribution manifold channels flow of the nitrous to the outer nitrous elements, maintaining pressure and mass flow across each element.
Extensive hand calculations from Atomization in Coaxial Jet Injectors, Lucien Vingert and Pierre Gicquel were used to validate atomization, and once the limits imposed by assumptions on surface energy and tension due to the multiphase, nonlinear, and non-deterministic characteristics of ethanol and nitrous, simulations began.
Above the injector face is a manifold that holds our two poppet valves. Much guidance has come from leaders like Julian Rice in the design and machining of these elements. These poppet valves are pneumatically actuated from their nominally closed state and have a PEEK cap to prevent mechanical damage and ensure a proper sealing surface, as they are inline. When the system is pressurized with CO2, the valves depress, allowing the flow of our oxidizer and fuel.
These valves, along with the Injector face and manifold, are CNC machined overseas. Like our bulkheads, these are tapped and bored for M5 bolts radially aligned.
Monte Carlo flow simulations were used across different temperatures and pressures of oxidizer and fuel in our tanks to understand the range of flow rates, and engine impulse, that may be seen.
On a high level, engine ignition is guided from a modified Estes C3 motor with an E-match, and Piranha line cutters actuate CO2 lines, which is controlled from our engine flight computer.
Finally, we use a Phenolic Ablative for the engine chamber with a Graphite throat, both of which have been CNC machined. A “Phenolic Retainer” block is bolted radially at the base of the Fincan, fixing these components into the control volume and against the Injector faceplate. The designs have been proven with hand calculations and simulation, and the control volume is maintained with O rings in the Phenolic. A neat design choice was integrating a 6061 sleeve around the midsection of the Phenolic (between its outer wall and the Fincan) that acts like a coffee sleeve — this is a secured air volume that lowers the temperatures seen by the Fincan through conduction with the engine chamber.
Values shown in our included .eng file come from HalfCatSim spreadsheets, with all inputs validated from simulations and industry guidance. Skoden shows an initial thrust of 3445N and an impulse of 17078 Ns, aligned with an N3115 classification.
All engine components have been designed, machined, and are in-hand awaiting cold flow testing components.
