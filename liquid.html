Skoden

A look at how Skoden is put together, from the full assembly down to the avionics and engine. Each part below includes an exploded-view animation showing how the pieces fit together.

Airframe

Skoden is a 5” OD rocket constructed of CNC Machined, Waterjet, and 3D Printed 6061-T6 Aluminum, G12 Fiberglass Bodytubes, and 3D Printed PA612CF and PA12GF airframe and support components.

A High Level breakdown of materials, methods, and manufacturing follows:

Beginning at the Nosecone, it follows a 3:1 Tangent Ogive geometry to prioritize strength over aerodynamics, and is 3D Printed with PA612CF filament with a Bambu X1E. It has a 6061-T6 tip with an M10

thread which serves 3 purposes; thermal protection, impact resistance, and a robust thread to integrate a threaded rod running to a titanium eyenut, mounted against a 6061 plate, used for recovery, ensuring that nosecone, and eyenut mounting, are as robust as possible during descent.

We then have a half metre of G12 fibreglass bodytube, cut in two portions, with control volumes on either side for main and drogue recovery.

avionics

Recovery systems are controlled using 2 DMR Blue Raven flight computers, each connected to two 12g EAGLE CO2 ejection cartridges. The avionics bay uses 2 fully redundant, separate power supplies for the EggTimer Mini GPS Transmitter and Trackers, as well as a 4K Hawkeye Camera facing downwards for footage capture. Tracking and transmission is split into two portions of RF design, using the Egg Timer Mini TX, paired with the RX + Redundant Battery systems and LCD-GPS Modules. These may be swapped by a featherweight altimeter later in the design cycle depending on team budget. Inverted Pursuits Labs pull pin switches will be used on each flight computer prior to launch.

engine

Skoden’s liquid engine uses a fuel centred coaxial shear injector with 44 elements. Stainless steel hypodermic needles are bonded to the injector with DP 420 epoxy, with the ethanol line running above, and a radial distribution manifold channels flow of the nitrous to the outer nitrous elements, maintaining pressure and mass flow across each element.
Extensive hand calculations from Atomization in Coaxial Jet Injectors, Lucien Vingert and Pierre Gicquel were used to validate atomization, and once the limits imposed by assumptions on surface energy and tension due to the multiphase, nonlinear, and non-deterministic characteristics of ethanol and nitrous, simulations began.
Above the injector face is a manifold that holds our two poppet valves. Much guidance has come from leaders like Julian Rice in the design and machining of these elements. These poppet valves are pneumatically actuated from their nominally closed state and have a PEEK cap to prevent mechanical damage and ensure a proper sealing surface, as they are inline. When the system is pressurized with CO2, the valves depress, allowing the flow of our oxidizer and fuel.
These valves, along with the Injector face and manifold, are CNC machined overseas. Like our bulkheads, these are tapped and bored for M5 bolts radially aligned.
Monte Carlo flow simulations were used across different temperatures and pressures of oxidizer and fuel in our tanks to understand the range of flow rates, and engine impulse, that may be seen.
On a high level, engine ignition is guided from a modified Estes C3 motor with an E-match, and Piranha line cutters actuate CO2 lines, which is controlled from our engine flight computer.
Finally, we use a Phenolic Ablative for the engine chamber with a Graphite throat, both of which have been CNC machined. A “Phenolic Retainer” block is bolted radially at the base of the Fincan, fixing these components into the control volume and against the Injector faceplate. The designs have been proven with hand calculations and simulation, and the control volume is maintained with O rings in the Phenolic. A neat design choice was integrating a 6061 sleeve around the midsection of the Phenolic (between its outer wall and the Fincan) that acts like a coffee sleeve — this is a secured air volume that lowers the temperatures seen by the Fincan through conduction with the engine chamber.
Values shown in our included .eng file come from HalfCatSim spreadsheets, with all inputs validated from simulations and industry guidance. Skoden shows an initial thrust of 3445N and an impulse of 17078 Ns, aligned with an N3115 classification.
All engine components have been designed, machined, and are in-hand awaiting cold flow testing components.
